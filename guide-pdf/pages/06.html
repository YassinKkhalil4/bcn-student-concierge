03 · Your first week Everyone

Eight things before any paperwork

None of these involve a government office. They're what makes everything else possible. Tick them off.

  • Ask your landlord or head tenant, in writing, whether they'll provide padrón paperwork, before you need it
  • Email your international office: do they run group or block TIE appointments for this intake?
  • Get working mobile data (home SIM roaming is fine for now)
  • Check your health cover is active in Spain and save the assistance number
  • Read your visa or authorisation and write down your registration deadline
  • Put passport, visa, insurance, enrolment and rental docs in one folder
  • Find your nearest copisteria (print shop) now, not at 8am on appointment day
  • Make sure you can receive money and pay rent from a working account
04 · Vocabulary Everyone

Four words people mix up (and one to photograph)

The most common confusion, and it costs people appointments. Having one of these does not give you the others.

WordWhat it isRemember
NIENúmero de Identidad de Extranjero
A numberYour foreigner ID number. Often already printed on your visa or approval decision.If it's already there, don't book a NIE appointment. A NIE does not let you stay.

A photo card · Non-EUProves your authorised status if you're authorised for more than 6 months.Fingerprints in person · €16.08 · EX-17
EU registrationCertificado de Registro de Ciudadano de la UE
A certificate · EUGreen paper certificate containing your NIE.Not photo ID: always carry your passport or national ID too · €12 · EX-18
PadrónEmpadronamiento
An address record · FreeRegisters where you live with the town hall. Needed for health services; often asked for at card appointments.It doesn't fix your immigration status.
ExpedienteNúmero de expediente
Your file numberOn your appointment confirmation and every resolution. Without it, nobody can find you.Quote it in every email or call. Photograph it the day you get it.
